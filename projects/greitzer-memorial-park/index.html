In 1963, George Greitzer Sr. lost his five-year-old son to drowning in the Susquehanna River. After the tragedy, Greitzer spent years advocating to transform the adjacent industrial dump into a safe recreational space for Johnson City families. Today, the park serves the community, but a lack of maintenance has led to deteriorating courts, inadequate drainage, and missing amenities.

George Greitzer Sr. died in 1979. Forty-five years later, the village has renamed the park (originally Boland park) in his honor and seeks to upgrade its aging infrastructure. By working with the Johnson City Planning Department and local residents, our Design Connect team led a community-driven redesign project through public charrettes, surveys, and board presentations.

The proposal introduces ADA-accessible pathways, new bathroom facilities, upgraded courts for basketball/tennis/pickleball, an expanded playground, and rain gardens for stormwater management. A memorial plaque honoring George Greitzer’s legacy anchors the design, which transforms the park into a thriving community node that connects residents through memory and recreation.

Contribution

As co-Project Manager and Public Engagement Lead, I coordinated community outreach with village partners, led weekly team meetings with delegated sub-team assignments, and produced GIS analysis maps, site plans, and renderings. Beyond management, I designed site circulation, a maintenance plan, and memorial elements. The work was compiled into a 55-page report and two posters that were presented to the JC Dept of Planning.

Ecology maps were made in collaboration with Julien Ricou, co-PM

Community Engagement

Our engagement process centered around 3 types of outreach: An in-person workshop charette to discuss ideas (which was featured on the local news), an online visitor survey, and a community board meeting presentation. Amenity data above was just a fraction of the feedback that we received, and our proposal made sure to address community desires. The iterative feedback loop between input and design laid a foundation for continued engagement during implementation. In addition to public outreach, we also presented our work internally to Cornell peers and faculty for the Design Connect mid and final reviews (left).
